How to tell it's really fluorescence
Idea. With medium only, everything the red channels see is LED leak, and it is a fixed fraction of the excitation channel (e.g. F8 = k × F6). The reference learns k. With cells, the app predicts the leak from the excitation actually reaching the sensor (k × F6 now) and shows what is left over: that excess is the candidate fluorescence. The fluorescence index is that excess divided by the excitation measured in medium, so it does not depend on gain.

Procedure. 1) Manual gain at the highest level where the excitation channel stays below 60 000 with medium. 2) LED off: capture a Dark at that gain. 3) Medium, LED on: Set leak reference. 4) Put the culture in, same LED: read Excess and the verdict (detected = more than 5σ and at least 10% above the leak).

Controls that prove it. a) Non-fluorescent absorber: a few drops of India ink or black food dye in medium, diluted until the excitation channel drops about as much as with your culture. Measured ÷ leak should stay near 1.0; whatever it shows is your false-positive floor, and the culture must clearly exceed it. b) Scatterer: diluted milk, same expectation (≈1.0). c) No excitation: with the 850 nm LED nothing is absorbed by the pigments, so there is no excess. d) Living signal: dark-adapt the culture 5–10 min, switch the LED on and watch the trend. Chlorophyll fluorescence changes over the first tens of seconds (induction); leak and absorption do not. e) Dose: a dilution series should give an excess that rises with cells and flattens at high density. f) Colour: the excess should sit in F8 (680) more than F7 (630), and should not appear at wavelengths shorter than the LED. Channels below the LED can still move with absorption, which is why a) matters.

In this transmission geometry part of the fluorescence is re-absorbed on its way to the sensor, so a front-face sensor would see more; a recovered excess here already shows the sensor can see it.
